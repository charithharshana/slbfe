Lesson 0008 — "Dugri" Directness & Workplace Communication Mastery

Mission link: In Israel, workplace culture uses a communication style called "Dugri" — meaning people speak directly, loudly, and say exactly what they mean without sweet or diplomatic words. If an Israeli supervisor speaks loudly or sharply, a weak candidate panics or feels hurt. Israeli recruiters intentionally test your mental toughness. This lesson trains you to stay calm, professional, and use exact clarification phrases instead of freezing in silence.

Strict Comprehensible Input mode: Follow Dilan through three high-pressure communication scenarios: receiving direct feedback on the warehouse floor, asking for clarification professionally, and maintaining confident body language under loud instructions. Comprehension precedes gentle speaking drills. Grammar rules are in the Optional Monitor Appendix.

Personal details used (real): Dilan Harshana, age 28, from Pahalagoda, Tangalle, emotionally mature, disciplined, trained to accept workplace feedback constructively and focus on high-quality execution.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson — through comprehensible input, not mechanical memorization — you will be able to:
1. Explain what the Israeli "Dugri" communication style means and why directness is a positive efficiency trait.
2. Use 3 professional clarification phrases instantly when you do not understand an instruction or interview question.
3. Handle direct, fast, or loud workplace instructions with zero emotional offense.
4. Avoid the fatal trap of silence or guessing during an interview.
5. Hold 2 core communication memory formulas: D.U.G.R.I. and C.A.L.M.

Estimated time: 40 minutes, split into 5 micro-blocks of ~8 min each.
Comprehensible Input level: i + 1 — communication psychology translated into ready-to-use verbal defense tools.

The Big Idea (read in English — visualize the dialogue)

During the interview, the Israeli representative asks a technical question very fast:
"Dilan, what's your exact plan if the barcode scanner disconnects during inventory cycle count?"

Dilan did not hear the last three words clearly. Many candidates would stay silent, look down, or guess wildly. Dilan does neither. He maintains direct eye contact, nods politely, and speaks with calm clarity:
"Pardon me, Sir. Could you please repeat the question?"

The interviewer smiles, repeats the question clearly, and makes a positive note: "Candidate has excellent presence of mind and asks for clarification without fear."

Israeli managers prefer a worker who says "Please repeat the instruction" over a worker who pretends to understand and causes an expensive mistake.

Micro-Block 1 — Pure Immersion: What is "Dugri"? (8 min)

CI principle applied: Pre-input immersion through psychological reframing ($i+1$).

1A. Scene 1 — The Warehouse Morning Briefing (4 min)

Read this story slowly. Visualize the working dynamic:

In Israel, work moves at a fast pace. Israeli supervisors value time, honesty, and efficiency.
They speak in "Dugri" (דוגרי) style:
- If you make a mistake, the supervisor will point it out immediately in front of you.
- They will not use polite filler words like "Maybe if you have time, could you perhaps fix this?"
- Instead, they say directly: "Dilan, this pallet is stacked crooked. Re-stack it straight now."

Dilan does not feel insulted or angry. He understands that:
1. Direct speech is not anger — it is honesty and efficiency.
2. They criticize the task, not the person.
3. Once the task is fixed, the supervisor smiles, offers a coffee, and says "Toda raba" (Thank you very much).

Micro-Block 2 — The 3 Gold Clarification Phrases (8 min)

CI principle applied: Repetition with variation across high-stakes dialogue drills.

2A. Scene 2 — When You Don't Hear Clearly (5 min)

Imagine you are in the interview room or on the factory floor. The air conditioning is buzzing, or the interviewer has a strong Israeli accent. You miss a sentence.

Never stay silent. Never say "What?" or "Ah?"
Use these 3 standardized professional clarification formulas:

  1. Formula 1 (Universal Standard):
    "Pardon me, Sir. Could you please repeat the question?"

  2. Formula 2 (When Sound Was Unclear):
    "Sorry, Sir, I did not hear clearly. Could you say that again, please?"

  3. Formula 3 (When Speed Was Too Fast):
    "Could you please speak a little more slowly, Sir?"

2B. The D.U.G.R.I. Resilience memory formula (3 min)

Letter Principle Mental Mindset
D Direct Speech Accept honest feedback without fear
U Understand First Ask for clarification if unclear
G Grow Stronger View corrections as skill improvements
R Respect Rules Follow company guidelines strictly
I Implement Fast Fix mistakes immediately with action

Micro-Block 3 — Handling Tough Feedback with C.A.L.M. (8 min)

CI principle applied: Emotional regulation and Affective Filter reduction ($i+1$).

3A. Scene 3 — The C.A.L.M. Response to Pressure (5 min)

An interviewer asks aggressively: "Why did you leave your previous supermarket job after a short time?"

A panicked candidate begins making excuses or complaining about previous managers.
Dilan uses the C.A.L.M. method:
- C (Composure): He takes a slow breath and maintains an upright posture with a subtle smile.
- A (Acknowledge): He acknowledges the question directly: "Yes, Sir."
- L (Logic over Emotion): He explains factually: "I completed my contract successfully and wanted to expand my retail skills and take on bigger responsibilities."
- M (Move Forward): He pivots to the opportunity: "This G2G program in Israel is the ideal long-term opportunity for me."

Micro-Block 4 — Interview Question & Answer Blueprints (8 min)

CI principle applied: Direct Q&A models using simple SVO structures.

Q1: "What is the 'Dugri' communication style, and how will you handle direct Israeli supervisors?"

Click to reveal model answer (SVO Structure) > **"'Dugri' means speaking directly, honestly, and saying exactly what you mean, Sir.** > If my supervisor speaks loudly or directly, I will not take it personally. > I understand it is part of an efficient work culture. > I will stay calm, listen carefully, and follow the instructions immediately."

Q2: "What will you do if an interviewer or supervisor gives you an instruction you do not understand?"

Click to reveal model answer (SVO Structure) > **"I will never stay silent and I will never guess, Sir.** > I will politely ask for clarification by saying: > *'Pardon me, Sir. Could you please repeat the question?'* > Once I understand, I will execute the task correctly."

Q3: "What will you do if you make a mistake on the job?"

Click to reveal model answer (SVO Structure) > **"I take full responsibility for my work, Sir.** > I will report the mistake to my supervisor immediately and fix it right away. > I believe in honesty and learning from mistakes so they never happen again."

Micro-Block 5 — Output & Self-Verification Drills (8 min)

CI principle applied: Gradual output release: Whisper drill → Cloze comprehension → Journal recording.

5A. The 3-Sentence Whisper Drill (3 min)

Whisper these 3 statements 5 times each with steady eye contact:
1. "Pardon me, Sir. Could you please repeat the question?"
2. "I respect direct feedback and always focus on quality work."
3. "I never stay silent — I ask for clarification politely."

5B. Cloze Comprehension Check (3 min)

Fill in the blanks with the correct communication terms:

    (Choices: Dugri / Whispering)
  2. If you miss a question during the interview, you should say: "__ me, Sir, could you please repeat the question?"
    (Choices: Pardon / Blame)
  3. Staying completely silent when nervous is a documented __-trigger.
    (Choices: pass / fail)

5C. 3-Words-a-Day Journal Entries (2 min)

Write these 3 words in your vocabulary notebook:
1. Clarification /ˌklærɪfɪˈkeɪʃən/ (Noun) = Making something clear or asking for repetition. (Ex: "I asked my manager for clarification.")
2. Composure /kəmˈpəʊʒər/ (Noun) = Calmness of mind and posture under pressure. (Ex: "Dilan maintained his composure throughout the interview.")
3. Feedback /ˈfiːdbæk/ (Noun) = Advice or constructive criticism about work performance. (Ex: "I welcome direct feedback to improve my speed.")

Optional Monitor Appendix (For Grammar Enthusiasts Only)

Note: Language acquisition happens naturally through understanding messages. Grammar rules below are for reference only.

  • Polite Modal Verbs: Use could and would for polite professional requests:
  • "Could you please repeat that?" (Polite) vs "Repeat that." (Too blunt)
  • Pronunciation of "Pardon": Pronounced /ˈpɑːdən/ (stress on the first syllable, gentle drop on the second). Never rush it.

කෙටි සිංහල පැහැදිලි කිරීම: ඊශ්‍රායල ජාතිකයන්ගේ සන්නිවේදන රටාව "Dugri" (කෙලින්ම, විවෘතව සහ පැහැදිලිව කතා කිරීම) ලෙස හැඳින්වේ. ඔවුන් උස් හඬින් හෝ කෙලින්ම කතා කළ විට එය කෝපයක් නොව කාර්යක්ෂම වැඩ සංස්කෘතියේ කොටසකි. ප්‍රශ්නයක් නොතේරුණහොත් නිහඬ නොවී ආචාරශීලීව නැවත අසන්න.

Micro-Block 1 — Pure Immersion: What is "Dugri"? ("Dugri" යනු කුමක්ද?) (විනාඩි 8)

භාවිතා වන CI මූලධර්මය (CI principle applied): මානසික ආකල්ප ප්‍රතිව්‍යුහගත කිරීම ඔස්සේ භාෂාව උකහා ගැනීම ($i+1$).

1A. Scene 1 — The Warehouse Morning Briefing (විනාඩි 4)

මෙම කතාව සෙමෙන් කියවන්න. වැඩබිමේ ස්වභාවය සිතින් මවාගන්න:

In Israel, work moves at a fast pace. Israeli supervisors value time, honesty, and efficiency.
They speak in "Dugri" (דוגרי) style:
- If you make a mistake, the supervisor will point it out immediately in front of you.
- They will not use polite filler words like "Maybe if you have time, could you perhaps fix this?"
- Instead, they say directly: "Dilan, this pallet is stacked crooked. Re-stack it straight now."

Dilan does not feel insulted or angry. He understands that:
1. Direct speech is not anger — it is honesty and efficiency (අවංකකම සහ කාර්යක්ෂමතාවයයි).
2. They criticize the task, not the person (විවේචනය කරන්නේ කාර්යය මිස පුද්ගලයා නොවේ).
3. Once the task is fixed, the supervisor smiles, offers a coffee, and says "Toda raba" (Thank you very much).

Micro-Block 4 — Interview Question & Answer Blueprints (ප්‍රශ්නෝත්තර ආකෘති) (විනාඩි 8)

Q1: "What is the 'Dugri' communication style, and how will you handle direct Israeli supervisors?"

ආදර්ශ පිළිතුර බැලීමට ක්ලික් කරන්න (Model Answer - SVO Structure) > **"'Dugri' means speaking directly, honestly, and saying exactly what you mean, Sir.** > If my supervisor speaks loudly or directly, I will not take it personally. > I understand it is part of an efficient work culture. > I will stay calm, listen carefully, and follow the instructions immediately."

Q2: "What will you do if an interviewer or supervisor gives you an instruction you do not understand?"

ආදර්ශ පිළිතුර බැලීමට ක්ලික් කරන්න (Model Answer - SVO Structure) > **"I will never stay silent and I will never guess, Sir.** > I will politely ask for clarification by saying: > *'Pardon me, Sir. Could you please repeat the question?'* > Once I understand, I will execute the task correctly."

Q3: "What will you do if you make a mistake on the job?"

ආදර්ශ පිළිතුර බැලීමට ක්ලික් කරන්න (Model Answer - SVO Structure) > **"I take full responsibility for my work, Sir.** > I will report the mistake to my supervisor immediately and fix it right away. > I believe in honesty and learning from mistakes so they never happen again."

නිවැරදි සන්නිවේදන වචන යොදා හිස්තැන් පුරවන්න:

  1. In Israeli culture, direct and honest communication is called __ style.
    (තෝරාගැනීම්: Dugri / Whispering)
  2. If you miss a question during the interview, you should say: "__ me, Sir, could you please repeat the question?"
    (තෝරාගැනීම්: Pardon / Blame)
  3. Staying completely silent when nervous is a documented __-trigger.
    (තෝරාගැනීම්: pass / fail)
නිවැරදි පිළිතුරු (Answer Key) 1. **Dugri** 2. **Pardon** 3. **fail**

5C. 3-Words-a-Day Journal Entries (විනාඩි 2)

ඔබගේ සටහන් පොතට මෙම වචන 3 එකතු කරන්න:
1. Clarification /ˌklærɪfɪˈkeɪʃən/ (Noun) = යමක් පැහැදිලි කරගැනීම හෝ නැවත පැවසීමට ඉල්ලීම. (Ex: "I asked my manager for clarification.")
2. Composure /kəmˈpəʊʒər/ (Noun) = පීඩනයක් හමුවේ මනස සහ ශරීරය සන්සුන්ව තබා ගැනීම. (Ex: "Dilan maintained his composure throughout the interview.")
3. Feedback /ˈfiːdbæk/ (Noun) = කාර්ය සාධනය පිළිබඳ ලැබෙන උපදෙස් හෝ විවේචන. (Ex: "I welcome direct feedback to improve my speed.")

Lesson 0008 — Dugri සන්නිවේදනය සහ වෘත්තීය ප්‍රතිචාර [කෙටි සාරාංශය සහ මතක සූත්‍ර]

ඉලක්කය: ඊශ්‍රායලයේ සෘජු (Direct) සන්නිවේදන ක්‍රමය තේරුම් ගෙන, පීඩනය යටතේ පවා සන්සුන්ව සහ පැහැදිලිව සන්නිවේදනය කිරීම.

ප්‍රධාන ඉගෙනුම් ඉලක්ක (Core Goals)

  1. Dugri (කෙලින් කතා කිරීමේ) සංස්කෘතිය තේරුම් ගැනීම.
  2. නොතේරෙන කරුණු නැවත විමසීමට වෘත්තීය වාක්‍ය ඛණ්ඩ භාවිතය.
  3. විවේචන හෝ උස් හඬින් දෙන උපදෙස් සන්සුන්ව භාර ගැනීම.

ප්‍රධාන මතක සූත්‍ර (Memory Keys & Formulas)

1. සන්නිවේදන සූත්‍රය: D.U.G.R.I.

කෙලින් සහ සෘජුව කතා කිරීම:
- Direct: බිය නොවී අවංකව කතා කරන්න.
- Understand: අපැහැදිලි නම් නැවත අසන්න.
- Grow: විවේචන ඉගෙනුම් අවස්ථාවක් කරගන්න.
- Respect: ආයතනික නීතිවලට ගරු කරන්න.
- Implement: වැරදි වහාම නිවැරදි කරන්න.

2. පීඩනය හමුවේ සන්සුන් වීම: C.A.L.M. සූත්‍රය

පීඩනයක් දැනෙන විට:
- Composure: ගැඹුරු හුස්මක් ගෙන සන්සුන් වන්න.
- Acknowledge: තර්ක නොකර "Yes, Sir" යැයි පිළිගන්න.
- Logic: කෙටි, සරල කරුණු සහිත පිළිතුරක් දෙන්න.
- Move Forward: විසඳුම දෙසට යොමු වන්න.

වෘත්තීය ප්‍රශ්න කිරීමේ වාක්‍ය (Clarification Formulas)

නිහඬව සිටීම හෝ වැරදි අනුමාන කිරීම පරාජයට මඟකි. මෙය භාවිතා කරන්න:

  1. "Pardon me, Sir. Could you please repeat the question?"
  2. "Sorry, Sir, I did not hear clearly. Could you say that again?"
  3. "Could you please speak a little more slowly, Sir?"

ප්‍රායෝගික පුහුණුව (Action Drill)

  1. Clarification Drill: ඉහත වාක්‍ය 3 මනසින් මවාගෙන හඬ නඟා 3 වතාවක් පුහුණු වන්න.
  2. C.A.L.M. සූත්‍රය: සම්මුඛ පරීක්ෂණයකදී අසීරු ප්‍රශ්නයක් ආ විට ඔබ සන්සුන් වන ආකාරය මවාගන්න.
  3. නිහඬ නොවන්න: "අපැහැදිලි නම් අසන්න" යන මූලධර්මය තදින් මතක තබා ගන්න.

දෛනික නව වචන 3 (3 Words a Day)

  • Clarification = පැහැදිලි කිරීමක් ඉල්ලීම ("I asked for clarification on the instructions.")
  • Composure = සන්සුන්කම ("He maintained his composure during the interview.")
  • Feedback = ප්‍රතිචාරය / අදහස් දැක්වීම ("I welcome direct feedback.")
