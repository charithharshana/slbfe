# Lesson 0004 — Supermarket Operations: FIFO & Floor Safety Mastery

> **Mission link:** In Israeli supermarket interviews, recruiters immediately test your practical retail skills. If you cannot explain the **FIFO method** or how to handle a **broken bottle / liquid spill** in English, the interview panel will assume your retail experience is fake. This lesson equips you to answer technical retail questions with total confidence using simple, fluent sentences.

> **Strict Comprehensible Input mode:** You will experience a day on the supermarket floor with Dilan across three vivid story scenes. Input comes first (reading and listening to stories), followed by context-based comprehension, mnemonics for permanent storage, and gentle speaking drills at the end. Grammar rules are kept in the Optional Monitor Appendix.

> **Personal details used (real):** **Dilan Harshana**, age **28**, not married, with **more than 5 years of supermarket and retail experience** (including three months of training at Keells Supermarket and more than five years working at a retail supermarket in Tangalle, plus forklift operator certification).

---

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson — through **comprehensible input, not mechanical memorization** — you will be able to:
1. **Explain the FIFO principle** (*First-In, First-Out*) clearly and demonstrate how to rotate products on shelves.
2. **Describe step-by-step spill and hazard protocols** using the **S.P.I.L.L.** method.
3. **Detail supermarket restocking procedures** from the warehouse (*machsan*) to display shelves using a pallet jack.
4. **Apply the H.E.A.T. customer service framework** when assisting shoppers.
5. **Differentiate cleaning chemicals and PPE** (surface sanitizers vs. heavy degreasers).
6. **Hold 3 core operational mnemonics:** **F.I.F.O.**, **S.P.I.L.L.**, and **H.E.A.T.**

**Estimated time:** 40 minutes, split into **5 micro-blocks** of ~8 min each.  
**Comprehensible Input level:** *i + 1* — you already understand supermarket work; this lesson provides the exact **professional English phrases** Israeli recruiters listen for.

## The Big Idea (read in English — visualize the scene)

> *The Israeli interviewer leans toward his microphone and looks directly at Dilan. He asks with a firm voice:*  
> **"What does FIFO mean, and what do you do when new dairy milk boxes arrive?"**  
>
> *Dilan does not panic. He takes a steady breath, smiles subtly, and replies in clear, simple English:*  
> **"FIFO means First-In, First-Out, Sir. When new milk arrives, I check the expiration dates. I put the new milk at the back of the shelf and pull the older milk to the front. This prevents spoilage and keeps products fresh for customers."**  
>
> *The interviewer smiles, nods, and ticks the score sheet: "Solid retail knowledge. Excellent English."*

Answering technical questions does not require complex academic vocabulary. It requires **exact, practical terms** organized in clear **Subject + Verb + Object (SVO)** sentences.

> **කෙටි සිංහල පැහැදිලි කිරීම:** ඊශ්‍රායල සුපර්මාර්කට් සම්මුඛ පරීක්ෂණයේදී තාක්ෂණික ප්‍රශ්න (FIFO ක්‍රමය, බිඳුණු බෝතල් පිරිසිදු කිරීම, රාක්ක පිරවීම) අනිවාර්යයෙන්ම අසයි. සංකීර්ණ ඉංග්‍රීසි අවශ්‍ය නැත; පැහැදිලි, සරල වාක්‍ය මඟින් ක්‍රියාවලිය පැහැදිලි කිරීම ප්‍රමාණවත් වේ.

## Micro-Block 1 — Pure Immersion: The Morning Restock (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Pre-input immersion through structured story narrative ($i+1$). Absorb the language naturally by visualizing the action.

### 1A. Scene 1 — The Milk Aisle at 7:00 AM (4 min)

Read this story slowly. Picture Dilan working in the supermarket aisle:

> At 7:00 AM, Dilan enters the dairy aisle. A large trolley of fresh milk bottles has just arrived from the supplier.  
> Dilan checks the **display shelf**. There are 8 bottles of milk already on the shelf with an **expiration date** of October 12.  
> The new milk bottles in the delivery crate have an expiration date of October 18.  
> 
> Dilan does not place the new milk directly in front. Instead, he systematically pulls the older bottles (October 12) forward to the **front edge of the shelf**.  
> Then, he stacks the newest bottles (October 18) neatly at the **back of the shelf**.  
> 
> A customer walks down the aisle, reaches out, and takes a bottle from the front. The customer gets fresh milk, and the supermarket avoids **spoilage and product shrink**.

### 1B. Key Observations (4 min)

Notice the three simple action verbs Dilan used:
1. **Check** → *I check the expiration date.*
2. **Pull forward** → *I pull older products to the front.*
3. **Place behind** → *I place new stock at the back.*

```
                     ┌────────────────────────┐
                     │     DISPLAY SHELF      │
                     │                        │
       [ FRONT ] ───►│  Older Stock (Oct 12)  │───► Sold First to Customer
                     │                        │
       [  BACK ] ───►│  Newer Stock (Oct 18)  │───► Moves forward tomorrow
                     └────────────────────────┘
```

## Micro-Block 2 — Story Context & Hazard Protocol (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Repetition with variation. The vocabulary is recycled through an emergency incident on the retail floor.

### 2A. Scene 2 — The Glass Spill in Aisle 4 (5 min)

> It is 11:30 AM. A customer accidentally knocks an olive oil glass bottle off the top shelf. The bottle crashes onto the tiled floor. Yellow oil and sharp glass shards scatter across the busy aisle.  
> 
> Dilan is 10 meters away. He acts immediately according to safety rules:
> 
> 1. **Secure the area:** Dilan stands near the spill and politely warns nearby shoppers: *"Please watch your step, Sir."*  
> 2. **Place warning sign:** He immediately places a bright yellow **"CAUTION: WET FLOOR"** warning cone in front of the spill.  
> 3. **Sweep glass:** Wearing heavy protective gloves (**PPE**), he sweeps up the broken glass shards with a dustpan and brush and disposes of them in a rigid bin.  
> 4. **Mop the liquid:** He takes a mop and uses a commercial floor **degreaser (D10)** to clean the slippery oil film.  
> 5. **Leave clean:** He allows the floor to dry completely before removing the caution sign.  
> 
> The aisle is safe, zero customers slipped, and business continues smoothly.

### 2B. The S.P.I.L.L. Safety Mnemonic (3 min)

| Letter | Action | Professional Interview Phrase |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **S** | **Secure** | *"I immediately secure the area to protect customers."* |
| **P** | **Place Sign** | *"I place a physical 'Caution: Wet Floor' warning sign."* |
| **I** | **Isolate & Sweep** | *"I isolate the glass and sweep it up safely with PPE gloves."* |
| **L** | **Liquid Mop** | *"I mop the oily floor with the correct cleaning chemical."* |
| **L** | **Leave Clean** | *"I ensure the surface is dry before removing the sign."* |

> **මතක තබා ගැනීමේ කෙටි ක්‍රමය:** **S.P.I.L.L.** = Secure (ආරක්ෂා කරන්න), Place sign (බෝඩ් එක තියන්න), Isolate/Sweep (වීදුරු අයින් කරන්න), Liquid mop (මොප් කරන්න), Leave clean (වේළුණු පසු බෝඩ් එක ඉවත් කරන්න).

## Micro-Block 3 — Customer Service & Hebrew Loanwords (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** High-frequency functional dialogue practice in realistic supermarket scenarios.

### 3A. Scene 3 — The Confused Hebrew-Speaking Customer (5 min)

> An elderly Israeli customer approaches Dilan in Aisle 2. She speaks quickly in Hebrew:  
> *"סליחה, איפה השמן זית?" (Slicha, eifo ha-shemen zayit? / Excuse me, where is the olive oil?)*  
> 
> Dilan does not know fluent Hebrew yet, but he stays calm and professional. He does not panic or run away.  
> He smiles politely, listens attentively, and catches the word *"Shemen"* (oil) or observes her gesturing toward cooking items.  
> 
> Dilan applies the **H.E.A.T.** method:
> - **H (Hear):** He listens carefully without interrupting.
> - **E (Empathize):** He nods with a polite, friendly expression.
> - **A (Apologize/Assist):** He says calmly: *"One moment please, Madam. Let me show you."*
> - **T (Take Action):** He walks with her to Aisle 4 and points directly to the olive oil shelf. If he cannot understand, he politely guides her to the Hebrew-speaking supervisor.

### 3B. Essential Israeli Supermarket Vocabulary & Loanwords (3 min)

| English Term | Pronunciation | Hebrew Retail Word | Meaning / Context |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Aisle** | `/aɪl/` (s is silent!) | *Ma'avar* | Customer walkway between shelves |
| **Back Storeroom** | `/stɔːr-ruːm/` | *Machsan* | Warehouse area where extra stock sits |
| **Display Shelf** | `/dɪˈspleɪ ʃɛlf/` | *Madaf* | Shelves where customers pick products |
| **Shopping Cart** | `/ʃɒpɪŋ kɑːt/` | *Agala / Kuppa* | Wheeled cart used by shoppers |
| **Pallet Jack** | `/ˈpælət dʒæk/` | *Ramesh / Jack* | Hand hydraulic lifter to move wooden pallets |
| **Degreaser** | `/diːˈɡriːsər/` | *D10 Chemical* | Heavy-duty kitchen/floor oil remover |

## Micro-Block 4 — Interview Question & Answer Blueprints (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Third encounter with target language in direct interview Q&A format. Simple SVO sentence structures.

### Q1: "Explain the FIFO method and how you apply it."
<details>
<summary><b>Click to reveal model answer (SVO Structure)</b></summary>

> **"FIFO stands for First-In, First-Out, Sir.**  
> It is an inventory method where older items are sold first to prevent spoilage.  
> When new stock arrives, I check the expiration dates.  
> I put the new products at the **back** of the shelf and pull older products to the **front**.  
> This ensures customers always receive fresh products and minimizes store shrink."

</details>

### Q2: "What immediate steps do you take if a liquid bottle breaks in an aisle?"
<details>
<summary><b>Click to reveal model answer (SVO Structure)</b></summary>

> **"My first priority is customer safety.**  
> First, I secure the area and put up a **'Caution: Wet Floor'** warning sign.  
> Next, I wear protective gloves and sweep up any broken glass.  
> Then, I mop the liquid using the proper cleaning chemical and degreaser.  
> I remove the warning sign only after the floor is completely dry."

</details>

### Q3: "What would you do if a display shelf is completely empty?"
<details>
<summary><b>Click to reveal model answer (SVO Structure)</b></summary>

> **"I replenish the shelf immediately.**  
> I go to the back storeroom (*machsan*) and bring the required stock using a pallet jack.  
> If the item is completely out of stock in the warehouse, I immediately report it to my supervisor."

</details>

## Micro-Block 5 — Output & Self-Verification Drills (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Gentle output progression: Read silently → Whisper drill → Out loud recording.

### 5A. The 3-Sentence Whisper Drill (3 min)
Whisper these 3 exact sentences 5 times each with steady pacing:
1. *"FIFO means First-In, First-Out — older items go to the front."*
2. *"I immediately place a warning sign to keep customers safe."*
3. *"I use a pallet jack to bring stock from the back storeroom."*

### 5B. Cloze Comprehension Check (3 min)

Fill in the missing technical words:

1. When restocking dairy items, older products are pulled to the ________ and newer stock goes to the ________.  
   *(Choices: front / back)*
2. In the event of a liquid spill, the very first step is to ________ the area and place a ________ sign.  
   *(Choices: secure / caution)*
3. To move heavy pallets from the back storeroom (*machsan*), workers use a ________ ________.  
   *(Choices: pallet jack / shopping cart)*

<details>
<summary><b>Click for Answer Key</b></summary>

1. **front** / **back**
2. **secure** / **caution** (or *warning*)
3. **pallet jack**

</details>

### 5C. 3-Words-a-Day Journal Entries (2 min)
Add these 3 words into your personal physical vocabulary notebook:
1. **Replenish** `/rɪˈplɛnɪʃ/` (Verb) = Re-fill empty shelves. *(Ex: "I replenish empty display shelves every morning.")*
2. **Expiration Date** `/ˌɛkspəˈreɪʃən deɪt/` (Noun) = Date when food spoils. *(Ex: "I always check expiration dates before stocking.")*
3. **Hazard** `/ˈhæzəd/` (Noun) = Danger / Risk. *(Ex: "Broken glass is a serious slip hazard.")*

## Optional Monitor Appendix (For Grammar Enthusiasts Only)
> *Note: You do NOT need to study this to pass the interview. Krashen's CI method proves that language acquisition happens through understanding messages, not memorizing rules.*

- **Action Sequence Connectors:** When explaining procedures, use sequence markers: *First... Next... Then... Finally...*
  - *"First, I secure the area. Next, I put up the sign. Then, I mop the floor. Finally, I remove the cone."*
- **Silent Letters Alert:** The word **aisle** is pronounced `/aɪl/` (the 's' is completely silent, just like 'island'). Never say "ice-le".
