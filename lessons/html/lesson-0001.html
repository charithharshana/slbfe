My First 60 Seconds — The 5-Step Self-Introduction

Your opening move in the interview. A short, strong, five-part speech you can deliver in under 60 seconds. Three views available — switch anytime using the tabs below, the keyboard, or the floating switcher.

3 interactive views 5 grammar patterns to lock in 4 number pairs to master 1 mission: deliver < 60 sec

Learning Outcomes GOALS

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  1. Deliver a fluent 5-step self-introduction in under 60 seconds.
  2. Pronounce the 8 critical number-pairs (13/30, 15/50, 14/40, 18/80) without confusing them.
  3. Substitute your own personal details (name, age, hometown, experience) into both template options.
  4. Recognise and use the core memory keys that recycle through the whole course.

The Big Idea HOOK

Imagine you walk into a small, bright room in Colombo. Three people sit behind a long table — two Sri Lankans and one Israeli in a dark blue suit. A camera on a tripod is recording you. One panel member smiles and says: "Good morning. Please, tell us about yourself." What do you say in the next 60 seconds?

Most candidates panic. Some say only their name and hometown. Others tell their life story for five minutes. The interview ends before they ever talk about their real skills.

In this lesson, you will learn one short, strong speech — a speech-with-a-skeleton. The skeleton has five bones. If you remember the bones, you can fill in your own words and never freeze.

Reading — The Model Speech INPUT

Read this aloud three times. Do not translate. Just feel the rhythm.

"Good morning, sir. My name is Nimal Perera. I am 28 years old, married, and I have one child. I am from Kandy in Sri Lanka. I have five years of experience working in the supermarket sector. In my previous role, I was responsible for stocking display shelves, performing FIFO stock rotation, and helping customers locate items. I also have experience handling cashier payments. I am a hardworking, honest, and highly reliable worker who always arrives on time. I am physically strong and comfortable with heavy lifting and working inside cold storage environments. I want to work in an Israeli supermarket to gain valuable international experience and earn money to provide a better future for my family. I am confident that my skills will contribute to your team. That is all about me, thank you, sir."
🔍 Quick check — answer from memory first
  1. How many years of experience? Five years in the supermarket sector.
  2. What does Nimal do inside the supermarket? Stocks shelves, rotates stock (FIFO), helps customers, handles cashier payments.
  3. Why does Nimal want to work in Israel? To gain international experience and earn a better future for his family.

Pronunciation — Numbers Are a Pass/Fail Trigger SOUND

If you say fifteen when you mean fifty, the panel stops trusting your numbers. Drill these four pairs every day for one week.

Number 1Number 2Trick to hear the difference
THIR-teen (13)THIR-ty (30)Say teen with a sharp "n" at the end. Say ty with a soft "ee" and "t".
FIF-teen (15)FIF-ty (50)Same trick. teen = sharp, high energy. ty = soft, lower.
FOR-teen (14)FOR-ty (40)Forty has no "r" before the "t". Many Sri Lankans say "for-ty" with an "r" — don't.
EYE-teen (18)EYE-ty (80)Eighty has a clear "t" in the middle. Eighteen ends with "n".

🎤 Drill — do this 5 times today, then 3 times every morning for 1 week

Repeat each pair 3 times aloud, then in a sentence:

  1. "I am thirty years old." (NOT thirteen)
  2. "I have fifteen years of experience." (NOT fifty)
  3. "My weight is eighty kilograms." (NOT eighteen)
  4. "I worked for forty hours last week." (NOT fourteen)

Self-recording tip: Open your phone voice recorder. Say the four pairs 5 times each. Play it back while walking. Your ear will catch your own mistakes faster than reading.

Grammar in Context PATTERNS

Look at the model speech. Notice these patterns — they appear in every interview answer.

Pattern 1 — "My name is ___."

Subject + verb + identity. "My name is Nimal."

Pattern 2 — "I am from ___."

Subject + be + place. "I am from Kandy."

Pattern 3 — "I have ___ years of experience."

Subject + have + number + noun. "I have five years of experience."

Pattern 4 — "I was responsible for ___."

Subject + was + adjective + for + verb-ing. "I was responsible for stocking shelves."

Pattern 5 — "I am ___."

Subject + be + adjective. "I am honest." · "I am physically strong."

Reading Practice — Two Templates CHOOSE

Read both options aloud. Pick the one that matches your real background — that is your home template for the next 12 lessons.

Template A — Candidates WITH Retail Experience

"Good morning, sir. My name is [Your Name]. I am [Age] years old, married, and I have two children. I am from [Your Town] in Sri Lanka. I have [five] years of experience working in the supermarket sector. In my previous role, I was responsible for systematically stocking display shelves, performing FIFO stock rotation, and helping customers locate items. I also have experience handling cashier payments. I am a hardworking, honest, and highly reliable worker who always arrives on time. I am physically strong and comfortable with heavy lifting and working inside cold storage environments. I want to work in an Israeli supermarket to gain valuable international experience and earn money to provide a better future for my family. I am confident that my skills will contribute to your team. That is all about me, thank you sir."

Template B — Candidates WITHOUT Direct Retail Experience

"Good morning, sir. My name is [Your Name]. I am [Age] years old, single, and live with my parents in [Your Town]. Although I do not have direct supermarket experience, I have [three] years of experience in logistics and warehouse operations. My daily duties included receiving goods, checking counts, and systematically arranging stock in the warehouse. I am a very fast learner and can adapt to supermarket duties quickly. I am physically fit, healthy, and accustomed to hard manual labor. I collaborate exceptionally well with multicultural teams, respect company rules, and am completely comfortable working night shifts and overtime. I am highly motivated to work in Israel to improve my professional skills in a developed country while supporting my family's economy. I believe I can do an excellent job for your supermarket. That is all about me, thank you."

Speaking Practice — Deliver It Now OUTPUT

Stand up. Look at the camera (or imagine an interviewer). Smile gently. Speak slowly and clearly.

💡 Pacing rule: Pause for 1 second after every sentence. Your brain needs that 1 second to feel calm.

Say it five times. Each time, change one small detail (your age, your city, your years) so you stop memorising and start owning the speech.

Templates දෙක (Two Templates) CHOOSE

Options දෙකම හයියෙන් කියවන්න. ඔබේ සැබෑ background එකට ගැලපෙන එක තෝරාගන්න — ඉදිරි lessons සඳහා ඔබේ home template එක වන්නේ එයයි.

Template A — Retail/Supermarket අත්දැකීම් ඇති අය සඳහා

"Good morning, sir. My name is Dilan Harshana. I am 28 years old, and I am from Tangalle in Sri Lanka. I have [five] years of experience working in the supermarket sector. In my previous role, I was responsible for systematically stocking display shelves, performing FIFO stock rotation, and helping customers locate items. I also have experience handling cashier payments. I am a hardworking, honest, and highly reliable worker who always arrives on time. I am physically strong and comfortable with heavy lifting and working inside cold storage environments. I want to work in an Israeli supermarket to gain valuable international experience and earn money to provide a better future for my family. I am confident that my skills will contribute to your team. That is all about me, thank you sir."

Template B — සෘජු Supermarket අත්දැකීම් නැති අය සඳහා

"Good morning, sir. My name is Dilan Harshana. I am 28 years old, and I live in Tangalle in Sri Lanka. Although I do not have direct supermarket experience, I have [three] years of experience in logistics and warehouse operations. My daily duties included receiving goods, checking counts, and systematically arranging stock in the warehouse. I am a very fast learner and can adapt to supermarket duties quickly. I am physically fit, healthy, and accustomed to hard manual labor. I collaborate exceptionally well with multicultural teams, respect company rules, and am completely comfortable working night shifts and overtime. I am highly motivated to work in Israel to improve my professional skills in a developed country while supporting my family's economy. I believe I can do an excellent job for your supermarket. That is all about me, thank you."

💡 Sinhala note

ඔබට supermarket අත්දැකීම් නැත්නම්, "fast learner" කියන පදය ඔබේ ප්‍රබලතම ආයුධයයි. "Although I do not have direct experience... I am a fast learner." — මේ structure එක මතක තියාගන්න.
