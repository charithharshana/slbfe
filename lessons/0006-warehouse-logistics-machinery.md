Dilan’s real qualification as a certified forklift operator is a major competitive advantage that must be articulated clearly and professionally in English.

> **CI principle applied:** Repetition with variation through a material handling operation.

### 2A. Scene 2 — Moving Cargo to the Sales Floor (5 min)

> Dilan grabs a manual **hydraulic pallet jack**. He slides the steel forks fully under a pallet of canned food.  
> He pumps the handle three times until the pallet rises 5 centimeters off the ground.  
> 
> He pulls the pallet smoothly toward the supermarket back storeroom (*machsan*).  
> When turning corners, he moves slowly and sounds a polite verbal alert: *"Caution, coming through."*  
> 
> In the high-bay storage zone, Dilan puts on his safety helmet and inspects the electric **forklift**:
> - He checks the hydraulic fluid and brake pedal.
> - He ensures the load capacity limit is respected (under 1.5 tons).
> - He lifts the pallet smoothly to the third storage tier without tilting.

### 2B. The L.O.A.D. Material Handling Mnemonic (3 min)

| Letter | Action | Professional Interview Sentence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **L** | **Lift Safely** | *"I insert the forks fully and lift the load smoothly."* |
| **O** | **Organize Stock** | *"I arrange pallets systematically according to item codes."* |
| **A** | **Align Neatly** | *"I align the boxes squarely so they do not fall."* |
| **D** | **Deliver & Secure** | *"I deliver the pallet to the designated aisle or storage rack."* |

## Micro-Block 3 — Industrial Packaging & Cargo Defense (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Technical domain vocabulary acquired in functional context.

### 3A. Scene 3 — Packaging Types & Pallet Wrapping (5 min)

> Before pallets are moved or stacked, they must be packaged securely to prevent goods from shifting or breaking during transit.  
> 
> Dilan uses four professional packaging methods:
> 1. **Stretch Wrap (Plastic Film):** Wrapping tight plastic film 5 times around the entire pallet from bottom to top to bind boxes together.
> 2. **Cardboard Corner Protectors:** Placing rigid cardboard edges on corners so strapping tape does not crush the boxes.
> 3. **Bubble Wrap:** Cushioning fragile glassware and sensitive electronics inside shipping cartons.
> 4. **Vacuum Packing:** Sealing perishable goods in airtight plastic bags to remove moisture and oxygen.

### 3B. The P.A.C.K. Cargo Mnemonic (3 min)

```
   ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                   THE P.A.C.K. SYSTEM                     │
   │                                                           │
   │   P ──► Protect Fragile Items (Bubble wrap)               │
   │   A ──► Align Box Corners (Straight stacking)             │
   │   C ──► Cover with Stretch Wrap (Tight plastic film)      │
   │   K ──► Keep Load Stable & Balanced                       │
   └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Optional Monitor Appendix (For Grammar Enthusiasts Only)
> *Note: Language acquisition happens naturally through understanding messages. Grammar rules below are for reference only.*

- **Action Sequence Infinitive of Purpose:** To explain *why* an action is performed, use *to + verb*:
  - *"I use stretch wrap **to secure** the boxes."*
  - *"I use a scanner **to verify** the inventory."*
- **Compound Nouns in Logistics:** When two nouns combine, the stress is usually on the **first noun**:
  - ***PALLET** jack*, ***BARCODE** scanner*, ***FORKLIFT** truck*, ***LOADING** dock*.
