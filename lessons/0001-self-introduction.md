# Lesson 0001 — My First 60 Seconds: Dilan's Story

> **Mission link:** This is your **opening move** in the interview. You will use these exact sentences in your first 60 seconds. Failure here = automatic rejection (see `01/interview_merged.md` §8.1, Don't #1).

> **Strict Comprehensible Input mode:** This lesson follows Stephen Krashen's Input Hypothesis and the 4-Week Plan from `01/info_final.md`. **Meaning first, grammar never as a barrier, low anxiety, lots of input, output only at the end.** The grammar section is hidden in an *Optional Monitor Appendix* at the bottom — open it only if you are curious, never required.

> **Personal details used (real):** **Dilan Harshana**, age **28**, not married, from **47/1 Pahalagoda, Tangalle, Sri Lanka**. He has more than five years of experience in the supermarket and retail field. He trained at Keells Supermarket for three months, and then worked at a retail supermarket in Tangalle for more than five years. He has also completed a forklift operator course and trained at **David Pieris Motors Company (Bajaj)**.

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson — through **input, not memorization** — you will be able to:
1. **Recognize** the 5 parts of your interview self-introduction when you hear them (input).
2. **Predict** the next sentence in your speech after hearing the first 3 sentences (comprehension).
3. **Whisper** your own 60-second speech with your real details (gentle output).
4. **Distinguish** the 4 killer number pairs when you hear them (input → recognition).
5. **Start** your physical 3-Words-a-Day vocabulary journal (Ramindu Randeni method).
6. **Hold** 6 mnemonics (anchors) that recycle across all 12 lessons.

**Estimated time:** 40 minutes, split into **5 micro-blocks** of ~8 min each, with 5-min breaks (per the glass-of-water rule, `info_final.md` Part IV §6).
**Comprehensible Input level:** *i + 1* — you already know basic English; this lesson adds interview structure on top of what you already know.

## The Big Idea (read in English — picture it in your head)

> *Imagine a small bright room in Colombo. Three people sit behind a long table — two Sri Lankans and one Israeli in a dark blue suit. A camera on a tripod is recording. One panel member smiles and says:* **"Good morning. Please, tell us about yourself."**
>
> *What do you say in the next 60 seconds?*

Most candidates panic. Some say only their name and hometown. Others tell their life story for five minutes. The interview ends before they ever talk about their real skills.

In this lesson, you will meet **yourself** — Dilan Harshana. You will hear your own story **three times** in three different ways. By the third time, the words will feel familiar — like a song you have heard before. Then, very gently, you will say it aloud yourself.

> **කෙටි සිංහල පැහැදිලි කිරීම:** Krashen කියන්නේ ඔබ **හැදෑරුම් වලින් නෙවෙයි, ඇසීමෙන් ඉගෙන ගන්නේ** කියලයි. ඒ නිසා මේ පාඩමේදී අපි පළමුව **ඔබේම කතාව කිහිප වතාවක් අහනවා**. පස්සේ ඔබම **නිහඬව** කියනවා. **වැරදි ගැන බය වෙන්න එපා — වැරදි තමයි ඉගෙනීම.**

## Micro-Block 1 — Pure Immersion (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Week 1 of the 4-Week Plan = pure immersion. **No notes. No speaking. No grammar. No translation.** Just listen. Your brain is doing the work.

### 1A. Watch — Don't Write (5 min)

Open YouTube and search for any of these (English subtitles ON):
- **"Israel supermarket interview English"**
- **"SLBFE interview sample"**
- **"tell me about yourself interview English simple"**

Pick **one** short clip (3–5 minutes). Watch it once with English subtitles. **Do not write anything.** Do not pause to copy words. Just **feel** the rhythm.

> මෙය ඔබේ **first input**. Krashen කියන්නේ මුලින්ම තේරුම ගැනීම ඇත්තටම ආරම්භ වෙන්නේ මෙතැනයි.

### 1B. Notice (3 min)
After watching, sit quietly for 1 minute. Then write down **3 things you remember** — anything. A word. A face. A feeling. A sound.

<details>
<summary>Why this works</summary>

Your brain is already doing what Krashen calls **acquisition** — building neural connections without you noticing. Even if you think you "didn't understand much," your subconscious has already stored patterns of English rhythm, intonation, and a few words. This is exactly how you learned Sinhala as a child — by **hearing first**, speaking later.

</details>

## Micro-Block 2 — Meet Dilan (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** New language is presented in **meaningful context** — like Krashen's German experiment (drawing Mr. Spock while saying "Kopf, Augen, Ohren"). We give you a story about **you**, with places, jobs, and feelings you already know.

### 2A. Dilan's Story (Read or Listen) (6 min)

> **Read this story out loud, slowly, like you are telling it to a friend.** This is **your** story. Don't translate. Don't analyze. Just feel.

📍 **A small town in southern Sri Lanka.** A young man named **Dilan Harshana** is packing his bag. He is **28 years old** and **not married**, from **47/1, Pahalagoda, Tangalle** — a coastal town in the south.

🛒 **At his work.** Dilan has **more than five years of experience** in the **supermarket and retail** field. He trained at **Keells Supermarket for three months**, and then worked at a **retail supermarket in Tangalle for more than five years**. Every day, he helped customers, arranged and checked stock, kept the supermarket clean and organized, and supported the daily operation of the business. He works carefully and quickly. When people need help, he helps them.

💪 **What kind of man is Dilan?** He is **honest**, **hardworking**, and always **on time**. People say he is the **most reliable worker**. His body is **strong** — he can lift heavy things all day. He is comfortable in **cold storage** and with **long shifts**.

🌍 **Why does he want to go to Israel?** Dilan wants to work in Israel because he wants to **gain international experience**, develop his skills, and build a better future. He is confident he can do the job well.

🙏 **End of story.** Dilan bows his head slightly and says: **"That is all about me, thank you, sir."**

### 2B. Picture This (2 min)

Close your eyes. Picture yourself in the story:
- 👨 **Dilan Harshana** (28, from Pahalagoda, Tangalle)
- 🛒 More than five years in supermarket and retail work
- 💪 Honest, reliable, strong
- 🌍 Wants Israel for international experience + professional development

<details>
<summary>Why pictures help (Krashen's German experiment)</summary>

When Krashen drew Mr. Spock while saying German words, listeners understood new words **immediately**. Pictures + words = comprehension. Your brain does the same thing with your own story. The story gave you **a person, a place, a job, a reason** — all the context you need to feel the English naturally.

</details>

## Micro-Block 3 — Three Encounters with Dilan (10 min)

> **CI principle applied:** **Repetition with variation.** Same facts about **you**, three different contexts. Each time the language gets slightly richer (i+1, i+2, i+3). This is how children acquire language — they hear the same story retold many times.

### Encounter 1 — Dilan Speaks to the Panel (the full speech)

> *Read aloud once. Imagine you are saying this to the Israeli panel.*

> *"Good morning, sir. My name is Dilan Harshana. I am 28 years old and I am not married. I am from Pahalagoda, Tangalle, in Sri Lanka. I have more than five years of experience in the supermarket and retail field. I trained at Keells Supermarket for three months, and then worked at a retail supermarket in Tangalle for more than five years. My responsibilities included helping customers, arranging and checking stock, keeping the supermarket clean and organized, and supporting the daily operation of the business. I have also completed a forklift operator course and trained at David Pieris Motors Company, which is a Bajaj company. I am hardworking, honest, reliable, and physically fit. I always try to arrive on time and complete my work carefully. I want to work in Israel to gain international experience and develop my skills. I am confident that my experience and positive attitude will contribute to your team. That is all about me. Thank you, sir."*

### Encounter 2 — A Friend Describes Dilan

> *Read this. It has the **same facts** but different words.*

> *"So Dilan is my friend. He is 28 and comes from Pahalagoda, Tangalle, in southern Sri Lanka. He has more than five years of experience in supermarket and retail work. He trained at Keells Supermarket for three months and later worked at a retail supermarket in Tangalle for more than five years. He knows how to help customers, manage stock, keep a shop organized, and support daily business activities. He has also completed a forklift operator course and trained at David Pieris Motors Company, a Bajaj company. He is not married. He is hardworking, honest, and reliable, and he wants to go to Israel to gain international experience and develop his skills. That is Dilan."*

### Encounter 3 — Dilan's CV (formal summary)

> *Read this. Notice the same facts in 4 sentences.*

> *"Dilan Harshana, 28, Pahalagoda, Tangalle, Sri Lanka. Not married. More than five years of supermarket and retail experience, including three months of training at Keells Supermarket and more than five years working at a retail supermarket in Tangalle. Completed a forklift operator course and trained at David Pieris Motors Company (Bajaj). Strengths: hardworking, honest, reliable, physically fit, and experienced in customer service and stock handling. Objective: a supermarket or retail position in Israel to gain international experience and develop his skills."*

### Comprehension Check (3 min)

After the three encounters, answer **without looking back**:

<summary>Where is Dilan from?</summary>

**Pahalagoda, Tangalle, Sri Lanka.**

</details>

<details>
<summary>How old is Dilan?</summary>

**28 years old.**

</details>

<details>
<summary>How many years of experience does he have?</summary>

**More than five years.**

</details>

<details>
<summary>Name three things he did at work.</summary>

**Helping customers, arranging and checking stock, and keeping the supermarket clean and organized.**

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does he want to go to Israel?</summary>

**International experience + professional development.**

</details>

<details>
<summary>What kind of person is he?</summary>

**Honest, hardworking, reliable, physically strong, on time.**

</details>

> 🎯 If you got 4 of 5 right, your brain has already acquired the facts. **Do not study the answers again.** Trust the acquisition.

## Micro-Block 4 — The 5 Bones Emerge (6 min)

> **CI principle applied:** We do NOT teach you the 5 parts. **You discover them** by looking at the pattern in Encounter 1.

### 4A. Find the Pattern (3 min)

Re-read Encounter 1 (Dilan's speech). Notice how his speech has **5 parts** that always come in the same order. Look at the brackets:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   1. GREETING + PROFILE                                      │
│   ──────────────────────                                     │
│   "Good morning, sir. My name is Dilan Harshana.            │
│    I am 28 years old and I am not married.                   │
│    children. I am from Pahalagoda, Tangalle, in Sri Lanka."  │
│                                                              │
│   2. EXPERIENCE                                              │
│   ──────────────                                             │
│   "I have more than five years of experience in the           │
│    supermarket and retail field. I helped customers,          │
│    arranged and checked stock, and kept the supermarket       │
│    clean and organized. I also have forklift training."       │
│                                                              │
│   3. STRENGTHS                                               │
│   ───────────                                                │
│   "I am a hardworking, honest, and highly reliable          │
│    worker who always arrives on time. I am physically        │
│    strong and comfortable with heavy lifting and working     │
│    inside cold storage environments."                          │
│                                                              │
│   4. MOTIVATION                                              │
│   ─────────────                                              │
│   "I want to work in Israel to gain international experience │
│    and develop my skills. I am confident that my experience  │
│    and positive attitude will contribute to your team."      │
│                                                              │
│   5. SIGN-OFF                                                │
│   ──────────                                                 │
│   "That is all about me, thank you, sir."                    │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Did you notice the 5 parts on your own?** If yes — that's acquisition. Your brain recognized the structure without being told.

### 4B. The Mnemonic Anchors (3 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Mnemonics are **memory aids**, not grammar rules. They help you recall what your brain has already acquired.

#### 🎵 Mnemonic #1 — **PESMS** (rhymes with "SMS")
| Letter | Bone |
|---|---|
| **P** | Profile |
| **E** | Experience |
| **S** | Strengths |
| **M** | Motivation |
| **S** | Sign-off |

#### 🤚 Mnemonic #2 — The 5-Finger Salute
Hold up your hand. As you say each bone, fold one finger:
- **Pinky** down = **P**rofile (who you are)
- **Ring** down = **E**xperience (what you did)
- **Middle** down = **S**trengths (what you are good at)
- **Index** down = **M**otivation (why you want it)
- **Thumb** down = **S**ign-off (close strong)

> ✍️ **Action:** Write "**PESMS**" on a small sticky note and put it on your phone. Every time you unlock your phone, say the 5 bones quietly in your head.

#### 🧠 Mnemonic #3 — The 3-Duty Rule
Dilan has **three** main duties at work. Picture them as a short list on his work badge: **Duty 1 • Duty 2 • Duty 3**. Three duties = three words on the badge.

> 🟢 This mnemonic will **recycle** in Lesson 5 (Supermarket Q&A).

## Micro-Block 5 — Vocabulary, Numbers & Your Story (8 min)

### 5A. 3-Words-a-Day Journal — Setup (3 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Ramindu Randeni's 3-Words-a-Day method (`01/info_final.md` Part II §2). Words are remembered only when learned **in their natural sentence**, not from a list.

📓 **You need a physical notebook.** Not the phone. Pen and paper — like in the German experiment, multi-sensory learning sticks better.

**Open it. Write today's date. Title it:** *Lesson 1 — Dilan's Story.*

**Now find 3 NEW words from Encounter 1, 2, or 3** that you did not know before today. For each word, write:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  WORD: _______________                                   │
│  MEANING (English): _______________________              │
│  FULL SENTENCE (copy from the encounter):                │
│  ___________________________________________________     │
│  ___________________________________________________     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> 💡 **Suggested 3 words** if you can't find your own (they come from your story):
> - **reliable** (*Dilan is a reliable worker — විශ්වාසදායක*)
> - **responsible for** (*I am responsible for… — මගේ වගකීම*)
> - **cold storage** (*the very cold room — සීතල කාමරය*)

#### Speak each word aloud **5 to 7 times** (Ramindu Randeni method). Your tongue is a muscle. It needs reps.

### 5B. Numbers as a Song — Listen First (5 min)

> **CI principle applied:** Numbers are **i+1** because Sinhala and English number systems differ in teen vs. ty. We treat them like a song — listen first, sing along, don't test.

🎵 **Mnemonic #4 — "Teen = sharp like a kid, Ty = flat like a calm grown-up"**
- **Teen** ends in **N** — short, sharp, energetic (teenager).
- **Ty** ends in **T** — soft, flat, mature (fifty is a calm middle-aged number).

#### 🎧 Listen-then-Sing Drill

Open Google Translate on your phone. Type each word, tap 🔊, listen, **then say it back**:

1. thirteen / thirty
2. fifteen / fifty
3. fourteen / forty (no "r"!)
4. eighteen / eighty

Now do it **with a sentence Dilan actually says**:

| Number | Sentence Dilan might say |
|---|---|
| **28** (twenty-eight) | *I am twenty-eight years old.* |
| **More than five** years | *I have more than five years of experience.* |
| **15** (fifteen) | *I worked fifteen hours yesterday.* |
| **50** (fifty) | *The crate weighs fifty kilograms.* |

🤚 **TPR game (Total Physical Response):** Stand up. Your helper says a number aloud in English. **Stand on tiptoes for "teen", crouch down for "ty".** This anchors the sounds in your body. (If alone, just nod up/down.)

> 🟢 This number drill will **recycle** in Lesson 3 (Number Power).

## Micro-Block 6 — Dilan's Story, Spoken by Dilan (Gentle Output, Low Pressure) (5 min)

> **CI principle applied:** **The Silent Period is sacred.** Krashen says: do NOT force early speaking. We move from read → whisper → speak — three gentle steps.

### Step 1 — Read your version (2 min)

Read the full speech aloud once, slowly. This version uses your real details.

> *"Good morning, sir. My name is **Dilan Harshana**. I am **28 years old** and I am **not married**. I am from **Pahalagoda, Tangalle**, in Sri Lanka. I have **more than five years of experience** in the **supermarket and retail** field. I trained at **Keells Supermarket for three months**, and then worked at a **retail supermarket in Tangalle for more than five years**. My responsibilities included **helping customers, arranging and checking stock, and keeping the supermarket clean and organized**. I have also completed a **forklift operator course** and trained at **David Pieris Motors Company (Bajaj)**. I am a **hardworking, honest, reliable, and physically fit worker**. I want to work in Israel to **gain international experience and develop my skills**. I am confident that my experience and positive attitude will contribute to your team. **That is all about me. Thank you, sir.**"*

### Step 2 — Whisper it (1 min)
Whisper the speech once. Whispering is between silent reading and full speaking — your vocal cords warm up without the pressure of being heard.

### Step 3 — Say it aloud (2 min)
Say it aloud once. **Nobody is recording you today.** Mistakes are NOT a problem — they are how your brain learns (Krashen's Monitor hypothesis says monitoring during speech causes anxiety; let it flow).

> 💛 **Affective Filter check (Krashen):** Did you feel tense? If yes, take 3 slow breaths before speaking. The lower your anxiety, the faster you acquire. **The interview panel is on your side** — they want you to succeed.

## 📓 Spaced Repetition — The 1-1-1-1 Lock (Ebbinghaus)

> **From `01/info_final.md` Part I §3:** Without review, ~50% is forgotten in 1 hour, ~70% within a day. Four reviews lock it in.

| When | What you do (5 min) |
|---|---|
| **+ 1 hour** | Read Dilan's speech once. Whisper your version once. |
| **+ 24 hours** | Say your speech aloud 2×. Open your 3-words journal, speak each word 5×. |
| **+ 1 week** | Time yourself — say your speech under 60 sec. Re-write the summary row of Cornell notes. |
| **+ 1 month** | Full mock with camera. Compare with today's whisper. |

> 🎯 Set four alarms in your phone labelled "**1-1-1-1 Lock**".

## Optional Monitor Appendix — Read ONLY If You Are Curious

> **Krashen's Monitor hypothesis:** Conscious grammar rules are useful **only** when you have time to think, focus on form, and know the rule. In real interview conversation, the Monitor **slows you down and raises anxiety**. So this section is **optional**. Read once. Then forget it. Trust the acquisition you have already done.

### Pattern A — "My name is ___." (identity)
- *My name is Dilan Harshana.*

### Pattern B — "I am from ___." (origin)
- *I am from Pahalagoda, Tangalle.*

### Pattern C — "I have ___ years of experience." (duration)
- *I have more than five years of experience.*

### Pattern D — "I am + adjective." (quality)
- *I am honest. I am reliable. I am physically strong.*

### Pattern E — "I am responsible for ___ing." (duty)
- *I am responsible for stocking shelves.*
- *I am responsible for handling payments.*

> 💛 **Do not study these patterns.** Your brain has already acquired them through the 3 encounters. Trust yourself.
