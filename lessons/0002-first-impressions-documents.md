# Lesson 0002 — First Impressions: Dilan's Perfect File

> **Mission link:** The interview decision happens in the **first 5 seconds**. Before you speak a single word, recruiters evaluate your **appearance, grooming, and document organization**. This lesson makes you look like a professional who respects the opportunity.

> **Strict Comprehensible Input mode:** You will meet Dilan preparing for his interview in **three different scenes**. Each scene recycles the same vocabulary with variation. By the third scene, the words feel natural. Grammar explanation is in the Optional Monitor Appendix.

> **Personal details used (real):** **Dilan Harshana**, age **28**, from **Pahalagoda, Tangalle**. He has a passport, NIC, birth certificate, school leaving certificates, and service letters from Keells Supermarket and his retail store in Tangalle.

<details>
<summary>Why appearance matters (The 5-Second Rule)</summary>

Research shows that interviewers form their first impression within **5 seconds** of seeing a candidate. This judgment is based entirely on **visual cues** — not your skills, not your experience, just your appearance. If you look unprofessional in the first 5 seconds, the interviewer assumes you will be unprofessional on the job. This is called the **Halo Effect** in psychology — one positive trait (neat appearance) makes the interviewer assume you have other positive traits (reliability, discipline, respect for rules).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why arriving early matters (The Calm Mind Principle)</summary>

Arriving early is not just about punctuality — it is about **mental preparation**. When you rush, your body releases **cortisol** (the stress hormone), which makes your mind foggy and your voice shaky. When you arrive early, your **parasympathetic nervous system** activates, calming your heart rate and sharpening your focus. Israeli recruiters specifically note candidates who arrive late or sweating — it signals poor planning and low discipline.

</details>

## Micro-Block 3 — The Mandatory Documents Checklist (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** **Repetition with variation** — you have now heard about documents twice (in the Big Idea and in Dilan's Morning). This is the **third encounter**, with more detail.

### 3A. The 7 Mandatory Documents (5 min)

Read this checklist aloud. These are the **exact documents** you must bring to your interview:

| # | Document | Why It Matters |
|---|----------|----------------|
| **1** | **Passport** (original + color copy) | Proves your identity and eligibility for a B1 work visa |
| **2** | **NIC** (original + color copy) | Verifies that your passport details match your national ID |
| **3** | **Birth certificate** (original + color copy) | Confirms your date of birth and family details |
| **4** | **School leaving certificates** (original + color copy) | Proves you completed 8–10 years of formal education (minimum requirement for manual labor visas) |
| **5** | **Service letter(s)** from previous employer(s) | **CRITICAL:** Must include company name, your full name, NIC number, exact job title, exact employment duration, company stamp/seal, and HR Manager or Store Manager signature |
| **6** | **Payslips** (minimum 6, spread across employment timeline) | Verifies your salary history and proves the service letter is authentic |
| **7** | **Marriage certificate** (if applicable) | Required if you are married and stated this in your application |

### 3B. The Service Letter Trap (3 min)

**This is the #1 reason candidates are disqualified after passing the interview.**

Your **service letter** must have these **exact details**:
- ✅ Company name (letterhead with official logo)
- ✅ Your full name (matching passport and NIC)
- ✅ Your NIC number (matching exactly)
- ✅ Your exact job title (e.g., *"Retail Sales Assistant"*, *"Packing Helper"*)
- ✅ Your exact employment duration (e.g., *"From 01 January 2019 to 31 December 2023"*)
- ✅ Company stamp/seal (official red or blue ink stamp)
- ✅ HR Manager or Store Manager signature (with name and title printed below)

**Why this matters:**  
Israeli representatives and SLBFE officers **call your previous employer** to verify your service letter. They ask:
- *"Did Dilan Harshana work at your company?"*
- *"What were his daily duties?"*
- *"What was his salary?"*
- *"Was he a reliable worker?"*

**If your interview answers do not match the manager's phone verification, you are disqualified immediately.**

<details>
<summary>Real example of verification call failure</summary>

**Candidate:** *"I worked as a machine operator for 5 years and earned Rs. 80,000 per month."*

**Recruiter calls the factory. Manager says:** *"He worked for 2 years, not 5. His salary was Rs. 40,000, not Rs. 80,000. He was a general helper, not a machine operator."*

**Result:** Candidate is disqualified for lying, even though he passed the English interview.

</details>

## Micro-Block 4 — The K.A.G.S. Entry Ritual (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** **Skill practice** through step-by-step rehearsal. You will practice this physically, not just read it.

### 4A. The 4-Step Entry Script (5 min)

When you enter the interview room, follow these **exact steps** in **this exact order**:

#### **K — Knock**
- Stand outside the closed door
- Knock **twice** gently: *tap-tap*
- Wait 2 seconds

#### **A — Ask Permission**
- When you hear *"Come in"*, open the door slowly
- Stand in the doorway and say: **"May I come in, Sir?"** or **"May I come in, Madam?"**
- Wait for the recruiter to say *"Yes, please"* or *"Come in"*

#### **G — Greet**
- Walk in calmly (do not rush)
- Stand in front of the chair and greet: **"Good morning, Sir"** / **"Good afternoon, Madam"** (use the correct time of day)
- Smile slightly (not a big smile, just a small, polite smile)

#### **S — Sit**
- Do not sit yet! Wait for permission.
- Ask: **"May I sit, Sir?"** or **"Can I sit, Sir?"**
- When the recruiter says *"Yes, please sit"*, sit down slowly
- Sit upright, back straight, hands on your lap
- Say: **"Thank you, Sir"** or **"Thank you for giving me this opportunity"**

### 4B. Physical Rehearsal (3 min)

**Practice this NOW:**

1. Stand up from your chair
2. Walk to a door in your house
3. Knock twice: *tap-tap*
4. Say out loud: *"May I come in, Sir?"*
5. Open the door and walk in
6. Stand in front of a chair and say: *"Good morning, Sir. May I sit, Sir?"*
7. Sit down slowly and say: *"Thank you, Sir."*

**Repeat this 3 times** until it feels natural.

> **මතක තබා ගන්න:** K.A.G.S. = **Knock, Ask, Greet, Sit**. මේක ඔබේ පළමු හොඳ හැඟීම සාකච්ඡාකරුවරයාට දෙනවා.

<details>
<summary>Why the K.A.G.S. ritual matters (Cultural Respect Signal)</summary>

In Israeli and international professional culture, asking permission before entering and sitting demonstrates **respect for authority** and **professional discipline**. Recruiters interpret candidates who walk in and sit down without asking as **entitled** or **disrespectful**. The K.A.G.S. ritual is a universal signal: *"I respect this opportunity, and I respect you."*

</details>

## Micro-Block 5 — The F.I.L.E. System: Organize Your Documents (8 min)

> **CI principle applied:** **Application** — you will now organize your own physical document file using the system Dilan uses.

### 5A. The F.I.L.E. Mnemonic (3 min)

Memorize this anchor for organizing your documents:

| Letter | Meaning | What It Means |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| **F** | **Fast Handover** | You can hand over your file **instantly** when asked |
| **I** | **Intact Originals** | No torn pages, no coffee stains, no crumpled corners |
| **L** | **Legitimate Payslips** | At least 6 payslips spread across your employment timeline |
| **E** | **Exact Dates** | Service letter dates match your interview answers **exactly** |

### 5B. Organize Your File NOW (5 min)

**Take action NOW:**

1. **Find a clean black or blue folder** (not a plastic bag, not a manila envelope — a proper folder with a cover)
2. **Gather your documents:**
   - Passport (original + color copy)
   - NIC (original + color copy)
   - Birth certificate (original + color copy)
   - School leaving certificates (original + color copy)
   - Service letters (original, with official stamp and signature)
   - Payslips (minimum 6, spread across your employment)
   - Marriage certificate (if applicable)
3. **Arrange them in this order** (from top to bottom):
   - Passport + copy
   - NIC + copy
   - Birth certificate + copy
   - School certificates + copies
   - Service letters (most recent first)
   - Payslips (chronological order, oldest first)
   - Marriage certificate + copy (if applicable)
4. **Use clear plastic sleeves** if possible, so each document stays clean and you can flip through them quickly
5. **Label the outside of the folder** with your name: *"Dilan Harshana — Interview Documents"*

<details>
<summary>Why document organization matters (The 2-Second Test)</summary>

Israeli recruiters conduct what is called the **"2-Second Test"**: When they say *"Give me your file,"* they start a mental timer. If you hand it over instantly and it looks organized, you pass. If you spend 20 seconds searching through a plastic bag, pulling out crumpled papers, you fail. The 2-Second Test is not about documents — it is about **work habits**. Recruiters assume that if you cannot organize your own documents, you cannot organize a supermarket shelf or a factory workstation.

</details>

## Optional Monitor Appendix — Grammar Notes (Open Only If Curious)

<details>
<summary><strong>Grammar Point 1:</strong> Why "May I" vs "Can I"</summary>

**"May I"** = formal, polite permission (asking if you are **allowed**)  
**"Can I"** = informal, asking if you are **able to**

In a formal interview:
- ✅ *"May I come in, Sir?"* (correct — formal and polite)
- ✅ *"Can I sit, Sir?"* (acceptable — slightly less formal but still polite)
- ❌ *"I can come in?"* (incorrect — sounds like a statement, not a question)

**Best practice:** Use **"May I"** for maximum formality and respect.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Grammar Point 2:</strong> Why "Good morning" vs "Good afternoon" vs "Good evening"</summary>

English divides the day into three formal greeting periods:

- **Good morning** = 6:00 AM to 11:59 AM
- **Good afternoon** = 12:00 PM to 5:59 PM
- **Good evening** = 6:00 PM to 11:59 PM

**Common mistake:** Many Sri Lankan candidates say *"Good night"* when entering an evening interview. **"Good night"** is only used when **leaving** or going to sleep, not when arriving.

**Correct:**
- ✅ Interview at 7:00 PM: *"Good evening, Sir."*
- ❌ Interview at 7:00 PM: *"Good night, Sir."* (wrong — this means "goodbye" or "sleep well")

</details>
