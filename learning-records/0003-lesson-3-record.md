## Key Learning Points

### 1. The Critical Number Pairs (Documented Fail-Triggers)
- **15 vs 50** (fifteen vs fifty)
- **13 vs 30** (thirteen vs thirty)
- **14 vs 40** (fourteen vs forty)
- **16 vs 60** (sixteen vs sixty)
- **17 vs 70** (seventeen vs seventy)
- **18 vs 80** (eighteen vs eighty)
- **19 vs 90** (nineteen vs ninety)

### 2. The Phonetic Distinction Pattern

**TEEN Numbers (13–19):**
- **Long "eeee" vowel sound** (hold it for an extra half-second)
- **Stress on the SECOND syllable**: fif-**TEEN**, thir-**TEEN**
- Phonetic pattern: `thir-TEEEEN`

**TY Numbers (30–90):**
- **Short "i" vowel sound** (quick, do not hold)
- **Stress on the FIRST syllable**: **FIF**-ty, **THIR**-ty
- Phonetic pattern: `THIR-ty`

### 3. Why This Matters (The Interview Failure Scenario)
If a candidate says *"I am **fifteen** years old"* when they mean *"I have **fifteen** years of experience"* but it sounds like *"fifty"*, the recruiter sees a 28-year-old face but hears "fifty years old" and concludes:
1. Candidate cannot understand basic English
2. Candidate cannot communicate work instructions
3. Candidate is nervous and unreliable
4. **Result: Interview ends early, automatic fail**

### 4. Dilan's Real Numbers (Must Be Pronounced Correctly)
- Age: **Twenty-eight** years old (not eighteen, not eighty-eight)
- Experience: **More than five** years (not fifteen)
- Previous salary: **Sixty thousand** rupees per month (not sixteen thousand)
- Height: **One hundred sixty-eight** centimeters (not one hundred eighteen)
- Weight: **Sixty** kilograms (not sixteen)
- Address: **47/1** Pahalagoda, Tangalle (forty-seven slash one)
- Work hours: **Fifty** hours per week with overtime (not fifteen)

### 5. The T.E.E.N. vs. T.Y. Mnemonic Table

| Number Type | Vowel Sound | Stress Pattern | Example |
|-------------|-------------|----------------|---------|
| **T.E.E.N.** (13–19) | **Long "eeee"** (hold it) | Stress on **second syllable** | fif-**TEEN** |
| **T.Y.** (30–90) | **Short "i"** (quick) | Stress on **first syllable** | **FIF**-ty |

### 6. Methodology: Input Before Output
The lesson strictly follows Krashen's Input Hypothesis:
1. **Micro-Block 1:** Pure listening (hear the vowel difference without speaking)
2. **Micro-Block 2:** Meaningful context (Dilan's number story)
3. **Micro-Block 3:** Minimal pairs discrimination drill (ear training)
4. **Micro-Block 4:** Recycled interview script (fourth encounter with the numbers)
5. **Micro-Block 5:** Output practice (recording, playback, self-correction)

Speaking comes **last**, only after the ear can distinguish the difference automatically.

### 7. The Minimal Pairs Drill
8 sentence pairs force the brain to actively discriminate:
- *"I am **fifteen** years old"* vs *"I am **fifty** years old"*
- *"I have **thirteen** years of experience"* vs *"I have **thirty** years of experience"*
- *"My salary was **fourteen** thousand"* vs *"My salary was **forty** thousand"*
- And 5 more pairs covering 16/60, 17/70, 18/80, 19/90, 3 months/30 months

### 8. Self-Correction Through Recording
Students record themselves speaking 8 critical sentences, then:
1. Listen back to their own recording
2. Compare to Google Translate voice pronunciation
3. Identify errors
4. Record again until satisfied

This builds **metacognitive awareness** of their own pronunciation.

### 9. The Whisper Drill
Whispering forces precise articulation without the distraction of volume. Students practice the 4 critical sentences in a whisper to build muscle memory.
